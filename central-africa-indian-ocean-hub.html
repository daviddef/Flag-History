Flags of Central Africa & the Indian Ocean

This hub is a deliberate grab-bag: four Central African mainland states and three Indian Ocean island nations that don't share one clean regional identity, grouped here mainly so the atlas has somewhere to put them. What does link them is colonial history — French rule across the Central African quartet's neighbors, a rare Spanish colonial holding in sub-Saharan Africa, and, on the islands, economies built first on enslaved and later indentured labor before each became fully independent between 1960 and 1976.

The four mainland countries — Central African Republic, Chad, Equatorial Guinea, and (by proximity and colonial history rather than this hub's own coverage) their neighbors — came out of French and Spanish colonization with long, difficult post-independence records: coups, one-party rule, and in a couple of cases dictatorships that lasted for decades. The three island nations — São Tomé & Príncipe, Comoros, Seychelles, and Mauritius — were uninhabited before European contact and were settled explicitly as plantation colonies, which shaped their demographics and economies well past independence.

About this hub

These seven countries don't share a single clean regional identity — four are Central African mainland states and three are Indian Ocean island nations — and this hub doesn't pretend otherwise. They're grouped together here mainly for atlas-coverage completeness: each was previously uncovered anywhere in this project, and grouping them into one hub was more practical than inventing seven near-empty hubs or forcing a false shared narrative. Chad in particular had previously appeared only as faint map-context in neighboring hubs, never as its own covered entry.

This hub also ships without the atlas's usual interactive map — the real-coastline, era-slider visualization that the Former Yugoslavia and other hubs use to show territory changing hands across centuries. That map, along with each country's own deep-dive flag lineage, is planned for a later, project-wide phase; this page is a lighter placeholder with real historical context above and one card per country.

Colors above are drawn from each country's current national flag, not a historical palette, since no per-country flag lineage exists yet. Once a country's own page publishes, its card here will link out.
