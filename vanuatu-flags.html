Flags of Vanuatu

Long before any European name was attached to these islands, the archipelago that is now Vanuatu held one of the densest concentrations of distinct languages and clan communities anywhere on Earth — scores of separate ni-Vanuatu societies, never united under a single crown or banner, spread across more than eighty islands. No flag is invented to stand in for that era; it is shown here honestly, without one.

From 1906 the islands were governed under a genuinely strange arrangement — Britain and France ruling jointly as the New Hebrides, not as one merged colonial government but as two complete, separate administrations running side by side — until independence on 30 July 1980 produced a single sovereign nation, Vanuatu, and the flag it still flies today.

Scope of this page: the islands of present-day Vanuatu, pre-1906 to present, three entries. No interactive map on this page — see the Pacific hub for the region-wide overview. Part of a larger atlas — see the linked pages at the bottom.

Independent Republic of Vanuatu

Independent state

Who & why: the New Hebrides gain independence from the Anglo-French Condominium on 30 July 1980, taking the new name Vanuatu — from a local phrase meaning roughly "our land forever" — and electing Father Walter Lini, leader of the Vanua'aku Pati, as the nation's first prime minister. Independence arrives only after a brief secessionist crisis on Espiritu Santo that year, put down with the help of Papua New Guinean troops shortly before the transfer of sovereignty.

What it represents: red stands for the blood of ancestors and national unity, green for the islands' richness, and black for the ni-Vanuatu people; the yellow band tracing a rough "Y" recalls the archipelago's own north-south shape, while the black triangle at the hoist carries a boar's tusk encircling two crossed namele-fern leaves — symbols of peace and traditional authority drawn from local custom.

1906 – 1980

New Hebrides (Anglo-French Condominium)

Condominium (dual colonial rule)

Who & why: the 1906 Anglo-French Convention formalizes a genuinely unusual arrangement: Britain and France govern the New Hebrides jointly, but not as one merged colonial government. Each power maintains its own resident commissioner, its own courts, its own police force, and its own currency, operating side by side across the same territory rather than blending into a single administration — ni-Vanuatu residents could, in effect, choose which of the two parallel legal systems to bring a case before.

What it represents: no condominium flag was ever officially adopted, since no single condominium government existed to adopt one; in practice, the Union Jack and the French Tricolore flew together over shared government buildings, each representing its own half of the dual administration — shown here honestly as a split flag rather than an invented joint design.

pre-1906

Indigenous ni-Vanuatu communities

Indigenous nations, pre-contact

Who & why: for millennia before European contact, the archipelago's Melanesian population organized itself into a great many distinct language and clan communities scattered across its islands — today reckoned among the most linguistically dense places on Earth relative to its population, with well over a hundred indigenous languages still spoken. No single overarching kingdom or federation ever bound these communities together; authority rested locally, in chiefs and clan structures specific to each island and often each village. European traders, missionaries, and, from the mid-19th century, "blackbirding" labor recruiters had contact with the islands for decades before the 1906 Convention formalized joint colonial rule, but that contact never produced one political entity capable of flying a single flag.

What it represents: no securely documented flag survives from this era, because none was ever needed or created — flags as a form belonged to the incoming colonial powers, not to the ni-Vanuatu communities themselves. Shown here honestly blank rather than invented, in line with this atlas's treatment of every pre-contact entry elsewhere.
