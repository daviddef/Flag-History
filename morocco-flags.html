Flags of Morocco

Morocco is the odd country out in this hub: the Ottomans never took it, and its ruling dynasty — the Alaouite sharifian line, on the throne since 1666 — is one of the oldest continuous monarchies anywhere in the world. From the early Islamic dynasties of the Maghreb through four centuries of Alaouite sultans, a divided French-and-Spanish protectorate, to a fully independent kingdom, this page traces four eras and the plain red-and-green banner that has flown, largely unaltered, since the early 20th century.

Morocco's modern flag — a red field with a green five-pointed star, the Seal of Solomon — dates only to 1915, added to an older plain-red Moroccan banner under Sultan Yusef during the French protectorate. It survived the protectorate era intact and became independent Morocco's own flag in 1956, unchanged since.

Scope of this page: the lands of present-day Morocco, c. 788–present, four political entities. Part of a larger atlas — see the linked pages at the bottom.

Kingdom of Morocco (independent)

Kingdom, independentMohammed VSunni Islam (Maliki)

Who & why: France and Spain end their protectorates in March and April 1956, restoring full sovereignty to Sultan (from 1957, King) Mohammed V and the Alaouite dynasty that has now ruled continuously since 1666, among the oldest reigning royal houses in the world. Tangier's international zone is reintegrated the same year, and Ifni and the Spanish Sahara follow in later decades.

What it represents: the same red-and-green flag adopted under the protectorate in 1915 carries straight through independence with no design change — a rare case in this atlas of a flag surviving the end of colonial rule completely intact.

French & Spanish Protectorates

ProtectorateMoulay HafidSunni Islam (Maliki)

Who & why: the March 1912 Treaty of Fez cedes control to France over most of the country, while Spain administers a northern strip and the southern Ifni/Tarfaya zones, plus an international zone around Tangier. Unlike much of French Africa, Morocco is never annexed outright — the sultan remains nominal sovereign throughout, with French and Spanish residents-general holding real power. Nationalist resistance builds through the 1940s–50s, and Mohammed V's exile in 1953 becomes a rallying point for independence.

What it represents: Morocco's own red flag gains its green pentagram in 1915, under Sultan Yusef, specifically to distinguish Moroccan vessels from other plain-red Ottoman and North African flags — it continues to fly as the sultanate's own banner throughout the protectorate, alongside the French and Spanish flags.

Alaouite Sultanate (independent)

Sultanate, independentMoulay al-RashidSunni Islam (Maliki)

Who & why: the Alaouite dynasty, sharifian descendants of the Prophet who established themselves in the Tafilalt oasis in 1631, formally take the Moroccan throne under Sultan Moulay Rashid in 1666. Unlike its Maghrebi neighbors, Morocco is never absorbed into the Ottoman Empire, remaining a fully independent sultanate that fends off Ottoman, Portuguese, and Spanish incursions along its coasts for centuries.

What it represents: a plain red field, a simple and very old Moroccan maritime and state color used by the sultanate long before any emblem was added — red banners are recorded flying over Moroccan ports and fortresses throughout this period.

Early Islamic dynasties of Morocco

Successive dynastiesIdris IIslam (Sunni Maliki from later dynasties)

Who & why: the Idrisid dynasty founds the first independent Islamic state in the far Maghreb around 788, followed across the centuries by the Almoravids, the Almohad Caliphate (at its height ruling most of Iberia and North Africa), the Marinids, and the Saadi dynasty — the Saadis notably repelling a Portuguese invasion at the 1578 Battle of the Three Kings. Each dynasty rules an independent Moroccan state; none is ever incorporated into the Ottoman Empire.

What it represents: no single flag can honestly represent nine centuries and five dynasties — heraldic banners of this era are poorly attested and varied dynasty to dynasty. Shown here honestly blank rather than invented.
