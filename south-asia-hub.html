Flags of South Asia

Eight countries across a subcontinent that holds some of the world's oldest continuous civilizations. The Indus Valley Civilization arose here around 3300 BCE, one of the earliest urban cultures anywhere, and over the millennia that followed the region produced major, well-documented empires: the Maurya Empire, which under Ashoka in the third century BCE controlled most of the subcontinent before he embraced Buddhism and non-violence; the classical Gupta Empire; the Delhi Sultanate; and the Mughal Empire, whose emperors built the Taj Mahal and ruled from the 1500s into the 1800s.

The British East India Company gradually displaced Mughal authority from the mid-1700s, and after the 1857 rebellion the British Crown took direct control, formalizing the British Raj in 1858 over most of the subcontinent. Nepal and Bhutan, the Himalayan kingdoms, were never fully colonized and remained independent through this period. Sri Lanka (as Ceylon) and the Maldives followed separate British colonial tracks. Afghanistan, further west, spent the nineteenth century repelling British invasions during the "Great Game" rivalry with Russia and retained its independence.

In August 1947, British India was partitioned into two independent states, India and Pakistan, divided largely along religious lines. Partition triggered one of the largest mass migrations in human history, as some fourteen million people crossed the new borders, and it was accompanied by widespread communal violence with an estimated death toll ranging from several hundred thousand to over a million. Pakistan itself was founded as two wings separated by a thousand miles of Indian territory; in 1971, after a brutal crackdown and a nine-month war, East Pakistan won its independence as Bangladesh. About this hub

This hub currently shows all eight South Asian countries without the atlas's usual interactive map — the real-coastline, era-slider visualization that the Former Yugoslavia and other hubs use to show territory changing hands across centuries. Building that map, including a careful treatment of the 1947 Partition and the ongoing India–Pakistan dispute over Kashmir, is a larger job than the rest of this phase allows, so this page ships as a lighter placeholder: real historical context above, one card per country, and full deep-dive flag pages and an interactive regional map planned for a later phase.

Kashmir, the Himalayan region at the northern edge of the former British Raj, remains a territorial dispute between India and Pakistan, administered in parts by each since a 1947–48 war shortly after independence; it is unresolved to this day and a fuller treatment is planned alongside the regional map.

Colors above are drawn from each country's current national flag, not a historical palette, since no per-country flag lineage exists yet. Once a country's own page publishes, its card here will link out.
