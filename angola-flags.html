Flags of Angola

Angola was one of Portugal's earliest and longest-held African colonies, its coast reached by Portuguese navigators in 1483 and not fully independent again until 1975 — nearly five centuries later. That long span includes real, organized African resistance: the Kingdom of Ndongo, under Queen Nzinga Mbande, fought and negotiated against Portuguese conquest for decades in the 17th century. Independence itself arrived only after a thirteen-year war, and was followed immediately by a civil war that ran for a further quarter century.

Scope of this page: the lands of present-day Angola, from the Kingdoms of Kongo and Ndongo through Portuguese colonization to the modern republic. No interactive map on this page — deferred to a future phase of the atlas. Part of a larger atlas — see the linked pages at the bottom.

1975 – present

People's Republic / Republic of Angola (independent)

Republic, independentAgostinho NetoState atheism, later secular

Who & why: Angola becomes independent on 11 November 1975 under the Marxist-aligned MPLA (Movimento Popular de Libertação de Angola). Independence is followed immediately, not eventually, by a devastating civil war (1975–2002) between the MPLA government and UNITA and FNLA rebel movements, heavily shaped by Cold War-era Soviet/Cuban and American/South African involvement; roughly half a million people are estimated to have died before a 2002 ceasefire finally holds. The MPLA has governed continuously since independence, transitioning from one-party Marxist-Leninist rule to a nominally multiparty system in the early 1990s.

What it represents: black for Africa and the continent's peoples, red for the blood spilled in the liberation struggle; the gold emblem — a cog-wheel for industrial workers, a machete for agricultural workers, and a star for internationalism and progress — deliberately echoes the hammer-and-sickle iconography of the MPLA's socialist allies.

1575 – 1975

Portuguese Angola

ColonyPaulo Dias de NovaisRoman Catholic (colonial establishment)

Who & why: Portugal founds Luanda in 1575, building on trading contacts along the coast that date to 1483, and gradually extends control inland over the following centuries against sustained African resistance — most notably Queen Nzinga Mbande of Ndongo and Matamba, who fought and negotiated against Portuguese expansion for roughly four decades in the 17th century. Angola becomes one of the largest sources of enslaved people sent across the Atlantic, a central and devastating fact of this era. Formal colonial administration consolidates in the 19th and 20th centuries as "Portuguese West Africa," and a war of independence runs from 1961 to 1974, ending only when a 1974 coup in Lisbon itself (the Carnation Revolution) brings down the Estado Novo regime and its colonial wars.

What it represents: the flag shown reflects Portugal's national colonial ensign of the later colonial period (green-and-red field with the national arms), flown over Angola as over Portugal's other African territories; earlier centuries used Portugal's older kingdom ensigns rather than a fixed territorial banner.

c. 1500 – 1671

Kingdom of Ndongo & Matamba (Queen Nzinga's resistance)

Kingdom, independentNjinga Mbandi (Queen Nzinga)Traditional/ancestral religion

Who & why: south of Kongo, the Kingdom of Ndongo rules the Mbundu people from around the 16th century. Its most notable ruler, Queen Nzinga Mbande, takes power in 1624 and spends the following decades fighting, negotiating with, and outmaneuvering Portuguese forces, at times allying with the rival Dutch against Portugal, relocating her base to the Kingdom of Matamba and continuing organized resistance until her death in 1663; Matamba itself holds out as an independent power into the 1740s before eventual Portuguese dominance.

What it represents: no securely documented national flag survives for Ndongo or Matamba; royal authority was expressed through regalia, drums, and symbolic objects rather than a fixed territorial banner in the European sense. Shown here honestly blank rather than invented.

c. 1390 – 1575

Kingdom of Kongo (northern Angola)

Kingdom, independentLukeni lua Nimi (founder)Traditional religion, later Catholic (Kongo court)

Who & why: the northern portion of what is now Angola falls within the Kingdom of Kongo's sphere from around 1390, the same kingdom that also spans parts of present-day DR Congo and Republic of the Congo. Portuguese contact begins in 1483 with generally cooperative early relations (including the conversion of King Nzinga a Nkuwu, baptized João I, in 1491) before Portuguese ambitions turn toward direct territorial control further south around Luanda from 1575.

What it represents: no securely documented pre-contact Kongo flag survives; after 1512, Kongo's Christianized kings did use a Portuguese-granted coat of arms in the European heraldic style, but no fixed national banner is attested for this earlier period. Shown here honestly blank rather than invented.
