Flags of the Pacific

No region in this atlas is more geographically dispersed. Long before any European ship entered the Pacific, Austronesian-speaking peoples had already settled it — a millennia-long expansion out of island Southeast Asia that carried Melanesian, Micronesian, and finally Polynesian voyagers across open ocean using wayfinding: reading stars, swells, currents, and bird flight without instruments. Polynesian navigators reached New Zealand, Hawai'i, and Rapa Nui (Easter Island), the three points of the "Polynesian Triangle," centuries before Columbus crossed the Atlantic — one of the great, underrecognized feats of human exploration.

European contact came late and slowly: Magellan crossed the Pacific in 1521, Abel Tasman charted parts of Australia, Tasmania, and New Zealand in the 1640s, and James Cook's three voyages (1768–1779) mapped much of the region in detail. Britain founded a penal colony at Sydney in 1788 that became Australia, and in 1840 signed the Treaty of Waitangi with Māori chiefs, still New Zealand's founding constitutional document. Germany, France, and the United States carved up most of the remaining islands as coaling stations, plantations, and strategic outposts. The great wave of independence came late too, mostly the 1960s–1980s, and several islands remain constitutionally tied to the powers that once colonized them.

About this hub

This hub currently shows all fourteen Pacific and Oceania countries without the atlas's usual interactive map — the real-coastline, era-slider visualization that the Former Yugoslavia and other hubs use to show territory changing hands across centuries. Building that map for a region this spread across the ocean is a larger job than the rest of this phase allows, so this page ships as a lighter placeholder: real historical context above, one card per country, and full deep-dive flag pages and an interactive regional map planned for a later phase.

Colors above are drawn from each country's current national flag, not a historical palette, since no per-country flag lineage exists yet. Once a country's own page publishes, its card here will link out.
