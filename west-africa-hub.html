Flags of West Africa

Sixteen countries, and some of the deepest pre-colonial history in the whole atlas. This stretch of coast and savanna held major, well-documented empires centuries before Europeans arrived in force: the Mali Empire under Mansa Musa (whose 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca, with a gold-laden caravan, is still the standard by which historians measure medieval wealth), the Songhai Empire that succeeded it and briefly controlled the Niger Bend's trade routes and scholarly centers like Timbuktu, the Asante Empire in the forest zone, the bronze-casting Benin Empire on the Guinea coast, the Oyo Empire's Yoruba cavalry state, and the Kanem-Bornu sultanate that endured for close to a thousand years around Lake Chad.

European contact began with Portuguese traders in the 1400s and hardened into full colonial rule after the 1884–85 Berlin Conference: France took the largest share (the federation of French West Africa), Britain held Nigeria, Ghana (the Gold Coast), Sierra Leone, and the Gambia, and Portugal held Guinea-Bissau and Cape Verde. Nearly all sixteen reached independence in the same tight window, 1957 to 1975 — Ghana first among them, Guinea-Bissau and Cape Verde last, after armed independence struggles. About this hub

This hub currently shows all sixteen West African countries without the atlas's usual interactive map — the real-coastline, era-slider visualization that the Former Yugoslavia and other hubs use to show territory changing hands across centuries. Building that map for sixteen countries at once is a larger job than the rest of this phase allows, so this page ships as a lighter placeholder: real historical context above, one card per country, and full deep-dive flag pages and an interactive regional map planned for a later phase.

Colors above are drawn from each country's current national flag, not a historical palette, since no per-country flag lineage exists yet. Once a country's own page publishes, its card here will link out.
