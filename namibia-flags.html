Flags of Namibia

Namibia's colonial history includes one of the 20th century's first documented genocides: the German colonial administration's 1904–08 campaign against the Herero and Nama peoples, in which tens of thousands died in massacres, forced marches into the desert, and concentration camps. German rule ended in 1915, but Namibia did not become independent until 1990 — three-quarters of a century later, after decades of South African administration, including direct apartheid-era rule, and a long armed liberation struggle.

Scope of this page: the lands of present-day Namibia, from Herero, Nama, and Ovambo societies through German and South African rule to the modern republic. No interactive map on this page — deferred to a future phase of the atlas. Part of a larger atlas — see the linked pages at the bottom.

Republic of Namibia (independent)

Republic, independent

Who & why: after a UN-supervised transition and elections won by SWAPO (the South West Africa People's Organisation, which had led the armed liberation struggle since the 1960s), Namibia becomes independent on 21 March 1990 under founding president Sam Nujoma — the last African territory to gain independence from colonial and settler rule (excluding Eritrea's later 1993 separation from Ethiopia).

What it represents: blue for the sky and Atlantic Ocean, red for the people and their heroism, green for vegetation and agriculture, white for peace and unity, and the gold sun for life and energy — a design that deliberately breaks from both the German and South African flags that preceded it.

1915 – 1990

South West Africa (South African mandate, then occupation)

Colony/occupation (contested)

Who & why: South African forces occupy German South-West Africa in 1915 during the First World War, and South Africa is granted a League of Nations mandate over the territory in 1920. Rather than preparing it for self-government, South Africa administers it as a de facto fifth province, extending its own apartheid laws there after 1948. The United Nations formally revokes South Africa's mandate in 1966, but South Africa refuses to withdraw, continuing what the UN and International Court of Justice both deem an illegal occupation through a long, bitterly fought SWAPO-led liberation war (the Namibian War of Independence, 1966–1990) before finally agreeing to a UN-supervised transition to independence.

What it represents: South Africa administers the territory under its own national flag throughout this entire seven-decade span; no distinct South West African territorial flag is adopted.

1884 – 1915

German South-West Africa

Colony (genocide)

Who & why: Germany declares a protectorate over the territory in 1884. When the Herero and later the Nama rise up against German land seizures and abuses in 1904, colonial commander Lothar von Trotha issues an explicit extermination order; German forces drive Herero communities into the Omaheke desert to die of thirst and starvation, and survivors of both peoples are confined in concentration camps marked by forced labor, starvation, and high death rates. Estimates commonly cited suggest that roughly 65,000 of an estimated 80,000 Herero and around 10,000 of 20,000 Nama died between 1904 and 1908 — widely recognized today as the 20th century's first genocide, a fact this atlas states plainly given how well documented it is. Germany formally acknowledged the genocide in 2021.

What it represents: the black-white-red horizontal tricolor of the German Empire's civil ensign, flown as the colonial flag across all German protectorates including South-West Africa.

Herero, Nama, Ovambo, and San societies

Pre-colonial societies

Who & why: before German colonization, the territory is home to the pastoralist Herero, the Nama (Khoikhoi-descended pastoralists), the agricultural Ovambo peoples of the north, and San hunter-gatherer communities, organized in clan- and chieftaincy-based structures rather than a single centralized state.

What it represents: no securely documented flag survives from this pre-colonial era for any of these peoples; shown here honestly blank rather than invented.
