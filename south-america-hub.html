Flags of South America

Twelve countries built on one real fault line: the Inca Empire — the largest pre-Columbian state in the Americas, centered on Peru — and other indigenous civilizations like the Muisca of Colombia and the Guaraní spread across Paraguay, conquered by Spain in the 1500s (Pizarro toppled the Inca in 1533) and organized into the Viceroyalties of Peru and New Granada / Río de la Plata. Portuguese Brazil ran on its own separate colonial track the entire time — still the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas.

Independence came as real war, not paperwork: Simón Bolívar and José de San Martín fought north and south through the 1810s–20s, and Bolívar's Gran Colombia briefly unified Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, and Panama before splitting apart within a decade. Brazil's own transition was comparatively peaceful — an 1822 declaration, then an Empire until 1889 before it became a republic. Guyana and Suriname sat outside the Iberian story entirely, held by Britain and the Netherlands and not independent until 1966 and 1975.

The twelve lineages

publishing next Brazil Portuguese America, a 1822–1889 Empire under Pedro I and II, then a republic — the hemisphere's one Portuguese-speaking giant. publishing next Argentina Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata, San Martín's 1816 independence declaration at Tucumán, decades of federalist-unitarist civil war. publishing next Chile The Mapuche frontier Spain never fully subdued, San Martín and O'Higgins's 1817–18 liberation, a long, stable republican run. publishing next Peru The Inca heartland and Cusco itself, seat of the Viceroyalty of Peru, the last Spanish stronghold before Ayacucho ended the wars in 1824. publishing next Colombia Muisca confederation, seat of the Viceroyalty of New Granada, the core of Bolívar's Gran Colombia before its 1830 breakup. publishing next Venezuela Bolívar's own birthplace and the first Spanish colony to declare independence, in 1811, then a founding piece of Gran Colombia. publishing next Ecuador Quito's own audiencia under Spain, briefly the third pillar of Gran Colombia, independent on its own account by 1830. publishing next Bolivia Named for Bolívar himself, carved from Upper Peru's silver-mining highlands, independent in 1825 as the wars' last new state. publishing next Paraguay Guaraní heartland and Jesuit mission country, an early and unusually self-isolated 1811 independence under Dr. Francia. publishing next Uruguay The contested Banda Oriental fought over by Spain, Portugal, Argentina, and Brazil, independent as a buffer state in 1828. publishing next Guyana British Guiana, not Iberian at all — a Dutch and then British sugar colony, independent from the UK in 1966. publishing next Suriname Dutch Guiana, traded to the Netherlands by the English in 1667, independent from the Dutch crown only in 1975.
