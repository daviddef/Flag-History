Flags of Somalia

No single Somali state existed before colonization: the Horn's own history is one of clan-based sultanates and trading city-states, from the medieval Sultanate of Adal's wars with Christian Ethiopia to the later Ajuran and clan sultanates further south. Colonization split the Somali-speaking coast between Britain and Italy, yet independence reunited it almost instantly — British and Italian Somaliland became sovereign within days of each other in 1960 and merged immediately into one Somali Republic, one of the fastest unifications in this whole atlas.

That unity did not last in practice: a 1969 coup installed a socialist dictatorship, and the central government's 1991 collapse left Somalia without a single, fully effective authority for decades. Its sky-blue flag, adopted in 1954, has flown through all of it.

Scope of this page: the lands of present-day Somalia, medieval era–present, five political entries. British and Italian Somaliland are treated as one combined colonial-rule entry, since both are covered by the same broad political category and unite within days of each other at independence; the union moment itself gets its own entry. Somali Democratic Republic → state collapse & federal republic

Civil war / state collapse

Who & why: General Mohamed Siad Barre seizes power in a 1969 coup and declares the Somali Democratic Republic, ruling as a socialist one-party dictatorship for two decades while pursuing (and losing, against Ethiopia in 1977-78) the pan-Somali dream the flag's own star alludes to. Barre's government collapses in January 1991 amid civil war and clan-based armed opposition, and Somalia has had no single fully effective central government since, cycling through transitional administrations, the breakaway (unrecognized) Somaliland republic in the north, and today's Federal Government of Somalia, established in 2012 and still working to extend real authority nationwide.

What it represents: the same 1954 flag continues in use throughout, regardless of who holds power — light blue for the sky, echoing the blue of the United Nations flag, and a white five-pointed star whose five points represent the five historically Somali-inhabited territories that pan-Somali nationalism hoped to one day unite: British and Italian Somaliland, French Somaliland (Djibouti), the Ogaden in Ethiopia, and Kenya's Northern Frontier District.

1960 – 1969

Somali Republic (union)

Union / Federation

Who & why: the British Somaliland Protectorate becomes independent on 26 June 1960, and the Italian-administered UN Trust Territory of Somalia follows just five days later on 1 July 1960 — the two unite immediately as the Somali Republic, a genuinely fast and distinctive unification among this atlas's country pages, achieved within days rather than years. Aden Abdullah Osman Daar becomes the new republic's first president. The union is not without friction — the two territories carry different colonial legal, administrative, and linguistic legacies (English/Italian, common law/civil law) that persist for years — but the merger itself holds until Barre's 1969 coup changes the state's character entirely.

What it represents: the same sky-blue flag with its white star, first adopted by the UN Trust Territory of Somalia in 1954 and carried forward as the flag of the whole united republic from the moment of union in 1960.

1884/1889 – 1960

British Somaliland Protectorate & Italian Somaliland

Colonial rule

Who & why: Britain declares a protectorate over the northern Somali coast from 1884-87, mainly to secure a provisioning route for its colony at Aden, while Italy establishes colonial control over the southern and central coast from the late 1880s-90s, formalized as Italian Somaliland by the early 1900s. Italian Somaliland becomes a UN Trust Territory under continued Italian administration from 1950, with independence promised within a decade — a distinctive postwar arrangement. Nationalist movements in both territories press for independence through the 1950s, timed so that both territories can become sovereign within days of each other in 1960 and unite immediately.

What it represents: the standard British colonial ensign pattern in the north; the Italian tricolor pattern (with a colonial badge, simplified here) in the south — two separate colonial administrations with no shared flag, reflecting the genuinely divided rule that independence and union would soon erase.

c. 1285 – c. 1800s

Sultanate of Adal, Ajuran Sultanate & successor clan sultanates

Kingdom / Sultanate, independent

Who & why: no single unified Somali state exists in this period; instead, real, substantial Muslim sultanates rise and compete across the Horn. The Sultanate of Adal, based around the port of Zeila, reaches its height in the 16th century under Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi ("Gurey"), who leads a prolonged and nearly successful war against the Christian Ethiopian empire. Further south and inland, the Ajuran Sultanate dominates much of south-central Somalia from roughly the 13th to 17th centuries, known for extensive irrigation and fortification works. Both eventually fragment into smaller successor sultanates — Geledi, Majeerteen, Hobyo, and others — that persist into the colonial era of the late 19th century.

What it represents: no flag survives with confidence for Adal, Ajuran, or their successor sultanates; this entry is shown honestly without an invented banner, labeled instead with the era's dominant polities.

Early Somali coastal towns & clan territories

Fragmented / pre-state

Who & why: before the rise of Adal, the Somali coast is already linked to Red Sea and Indian Ocean trade through towns like Zeila and Mogadishu, itself founded by Arab and Persian settlers by roughly the 9th-10th century, while the interior is organized by clan rather than any single central authority. This is the deepest, least documented layer of Somalia's own timeline, included here for completeness rather than as a claim of any single governing flag.

What it represents: no securely documented flag survives from this era for any Somali coastal town or clan territory. Shown here honestly blank rather than invented.
