Flags of East Africa

Eleven countries with some of the most distinctive statehoods on the continent. Ethiopia is Africa's oldest continuously independent state — the Aksumite Kingdom, then the Ethiopian Empire, never colonized except a brief 1936–41 Italian occupation. Along the coast, the Sultanate of Zanzibar and Swahili city-states like Kilwa and Mombasa built a real, distinct Indian Ocean trading civilization, linking East Africa to Arabia, Persia, and India centuries before Europeans arrived.

Everywhere else, the 1880s–1900s "Scramble for Africa" carved the region among Britain, Germany, Italy, and France. Most of the region won independence in the early-to-mid 1960s, but two moments came much later and stand on their own: Eritrea's 1993 independence from Ethiopia after a thirty-year war, and South Sudan's 2011 independence from Sudan — the world's newest country. Individual country pages, with full flag timelines like the rest of the atlas, are publishing next.

The eleven lineages

publishing next Kenya The Mau Mau uprising against British rule, independence in 1963 under Jomo Kenyatta, and a coast shaped by Swahili trading city-states. publishing next Tanzania German East Africa, then British Tanganyika, uniting with the island Sultanate of Zanzibar in 1964 to form modern Tanzania. publishing next Ethiopia Africa's oldest continuously independent state — the Aksumite Kingdom through the Ethiopian Empire, never colonized except a 1936–41 Italian occupation. publishing next Uganda The Kingdom of Buganda's precolonial power, a British protectorate from 1894, and independence in 1962. publishing next Rwanda The old Kingdom of Rwanda, Belgian trusteeship after the First World War, and the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi the country has spent three decades rebuilding from. publishing next Burundi A centuries-old kingdom, joined with Rwanda under Belgian rule as Ruanda-Urundi, independence in 1962, and its own monarchy dissolved in 1966. publishing next Somalia British and Italian Somaliland united at independence in 1960; central state authority collapsed after 1991, and the country has been rebuilding it since. publishing next Eritrea Italy's first African colony from the 1880s, later federated with and then annexed by Ethiopia, and a thirty-year war that won independence in 1993. publishing next Djibouti French Somaliland at the mouth of the Red Sea, held onto by France through the region's wave of decolonization, independent only in 1977. publishing next South Sudan Decades of civil war against Khartoum ended in a 2011 referendum that made it the world's newest country. publishing next Madagascar An Austronesian-heritage population unlike its African neighbors, the Kingdom of Imerina's unification of the island, and French colonization from 1897.

About this atlas

This started as a family history project: tracing the flags that flew over the lands our ancestors came from, close enough to paint accurately — correct colors, correct proportions, correct dates. East Africa joins the atlas as its own hub because its history stands apart from the rest of the project so far: one country here, Ethiopia, was never colonized in any lasting sense, while a Swahili-coast trading civilization linked this region to the Indian Ocean world centuries before the European "Scramble for Africa" arrived.

This is a deliberately lighter hub page for now — a placeholder grid of the eleven lineages rather than the full interactive map built for earlier hubs like Former Yugoslavia or Scandinavia. A later phase will add a real traced map, time slider, and flag-by-flag timeline to this region, matching that fuller template. Colors above are provisional swatches, not final flag specifications.
